Fall 2026

I hope everyone is having a great end of their summer. Here at BUMA, we've been hard at work trying to make the coming school year something special. Here's what's new this year, and a peek into our upcoming schedule.

General Meetings

This semester, our general meetings will be in CDS 548, from 5-6pm. We have a tentative schedule posted on our events page, but we're still open to modifying our schedule. If there's anything you're interested in seeing, get in contact with us, or one of our board members.

Our first event will be a game night! The goal of this event is to get to know other people in the math community, and to welcome new members from Splash. We'll bring some board games and card games (feel free to bring your own), but the main focus is just to hang out and make some friends, with a light focus on math. We'll get more into the math stuff in our second meeting, celebrating the 2026 Fields medalists, as well as Jean-Pierre Serre's 100th birthday!

Putnam Practice Problem Sessions

Returning with the fall semester are our Putnam practice problem sessions! These are weekly events where a professor prepares a list of Putnam-style problems, often from past Putnam exams, and we try to solve them together. These sessions are great practice if you intend on taking the Putnam exam this fall. Even if you don't intend to take the exam, feel free to show up and just have some fun solving difficult problems.

For those unaware, the Putnam Exam is a prestigious competitive math exam for college students. The exam itself is divided into two 3 hour sections, each for 6 problems, which are worth 10 points each. The median score is usually 0/120 or 1/120. Those are not typos. The problems are incredibly creative and difficult, but are designed to be accessible to almost all university mathematics students, and only require knowledge from lower level math courses like calculus, analysis, linear algebra, and abstract algebra.

This year, they will be hosted in collaboration with Professor David Fried, while Professor Jared Weinstein is on sabbatical. They will be hosted from 5:30-7:30pm on Mondays, starting on September 14th, in CDS 365. See our events page for more details.

Pizza and Food at Meetings

Those who've been around for a while know that we used to order Domino's pizza for all our meetings, but last semester, we weren't able to do this due to new SLIC guidelines prohibiting food in classrooms. Since SLIC will not approve us having food, we are unable to submit a request to use club funds to purchase pizza.

We're working with the math department to see if we can get an exception to this rule since our rooms are managed by the math department, which historically hasn't cared about this guideline. We likely won't have food for our first few meetings, but we are hopeful that we will be able to have pizza for at least our bigger meetings. We'll keep everyone updated on the status of this issue.

E-Board Status

Of the 5 members of our E-Board, four of them are seniors, and the other one will be leaving the E-Board voluntarily next year to help out a different club. As such, we will be recruiting and training new E-Board members to keep the club going after we are gone. More details will be announced a few weeks into the semester.

Because most of our E-Board is leaving in May, we all want to make this year special. We've already got a lot of great ideas for how to accomplish this, so make sure to stick around because this year will be awesome.

Theorem of the Week

We decided to add a Theorem of the Week to our homepage, which will showcase some cool math facts, especially those you're unlikely to encounter in your classes. The Theorem of the Week will be updated every Sunday at 12:00am, and a discussion of the Theorem of the Week will also be posted on our posts page, at the same time.

Our discussion posts will introduce ideas from fields of math you may not have seen before, will discuss the theorem itself and its proof, and will discuss some of its applications. We want to make these posts accessible to everyone, regardless of mathematical background.

If you know a particular theorem that you want featured, you can fill out this form to request that we feature your theorem! If you want, you can even write the discussion post yourself. If we use your suggestion, we will feature your name on the theorem when we post it!

Status of the Society of Mathematics

Many of you are aware that last semester, we entered a formal merger with the Society of Mathematics (SoM), another math club here at BU. They did not have SLIC recognition, which prohibits them from doing simple things like booking rooms, and the easiest way for us to help them out was to formally incorporate their club as part of our club. This did not affect either of our club's operations, and was simply a formal action meant to benefit another club.

This merger is still in effect this year. The leadership of SoM has fully changed this year, and we're trying to work much more collaboratively with them. We invite all of our members to visit their events.

SoM hosts the BU Undergraduate Colloquium, which is a series of talks given by undergraduate students at BU. The goal of the Colloquium is to provide students a venue for giving talks on topics they find interesting, with a general focus on pure mathematics. Oftentimes very advanced topics are presented here in accessible ways, so it's a great place to learn something new. Many of our members also attend (and even give talks at) the Colloquium, so feel free to swing by sometime.

This year, the Colloquium will be held on Fridays in CDS 365 from 6-8pm, and the topic this semester is Symmetries and Invariants. More details can be found in the SoM Discord server, which is linked on their website.

What Are These Newsletters?

These newsletters are our way to be more transparent and open with the community about what's happening at BUMA. We really wanted to share all the stuff we're working on, and so we've decided to start posting these newsletters for everyone to see.

These will be posted every other week, and will probably be significantly shorter than this first major update post (with a few exceptions).

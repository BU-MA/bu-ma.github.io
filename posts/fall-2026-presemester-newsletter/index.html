Grant Talbert · August 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Fall 2026

I hope everyone is having a great end of their summer. Here at BUMA, we're hard at work trying to make the coming school year something special. Here's what's new this year, and a peek into our upcoming schedule.

Putnam Practice Problem Sessions

Returning with the fall semester is our Putnam practice problem sessions! These are weekly events where a professor prepares a list of Putnam-style problems, often coming from past Putnam exams, and we get together to try and solve them together. These sessions are great practice if you intend on taking the Putnam exam this fall. Even if you don't intend to take the exam, feel free to show up and just have some fun solving difficult problems.

For those unaware, the Putnam Exam is a prestigious competitive math exam for college students. The exam itself is divided into two 3 hour sections, each for 6 problems, which are worth 10 points each. The median score is usually 0/120 or 1/120. Those are not typos. The problems are incredibly creative and difficult, but are designed to be accessible to almost all university mathematics students, and only require knowledge from lower level math courses like calculus, analysis, linear algebra, and abstract algebra.

This year, they will be hosted in collaboration with Professor David Fried, while Professor Jared Weinstein is on sabbatical. They will be hosted from 5:30-7:30pm on Mondays, starting on September 14th, in CDS 365. See our events page for more details.

Pizza and Food at Meetings

Those who've been around for a while know that we used to order Domino's pizza for all our meetings, but last semester, we weren't able to do this due to new SLIC guidelines prohibiting food in classrooms. Since SLIC will not approve us having food, we are unable to submit a request to use club funds to purchase pizza.

We're working with the math department to see if we can get an exception to this rule, since our rooms are managed by the math department, who historically hasn't cared about this guideline. We likely won't have food for our first two or three meetings, but we are hopeful that we will be able to have pizza for all our following meetings. We'll keep everyone updated on the status of this issue.

E-Board Status

Of the 6 members of our E-Board, 4 are seniors, 1 is a grad student who does not have much time to commit to our club, and the last one will be leaving the E-Board voluntarily next semester, to help out a different club. As such, we will be recruiting and training new E-Board members to keep the club going after we are gone. More details will be announced a couple weeks into the semester.

Because most of our E-Board is leaving after this year, we all want to make this year special. We've already got a lot of great ideas for how to accomplish this, and the school year hasn't even started yet, so make sure to stick around because this year will be awesome.

Theorem & Putnam Problem of the Week

We wanted to add either a Theorem of the Week or a Putnam Problem of the Week, but we didn't know which one to choose, so we just added both! Both of these are posted on our homepage, and will automatically update every Sunday at 12:00am EST.

A solution to the Putnam Problem of the Week will be posted on Saturday at 12:00am EST on our posts page. You can submit solutions to the Putnam Problem of the Week via a Google Form that will be linked in the problem itself. If your solution is correct, your name will be featured in our solution post! We may even feature your solution as well, if it's interesting and differs from the solution we have prepared.

A discussion of the Theorem of the Week will also be posted on our posts page, at the same time the Theorem of the Week itself is updated. We want to use the Theorem of the Week to showcase some cool math facts, especially those you're unlikely to encounter in your classes. Our discussion posts will introduce ideas from fields of math you may not have seen before, and will discuss the theorem itself, as well as its applications. We want to make these posts accessible to everyone, regardless of mathematical background.

If you know a particular theorem that you want featured, you can fill out this form to request that we feature your theorem! If you want, you can even write the discussion post yourself. If we use your suggestion, we will feature your name on the theorem when we post it!

Status of the Society of Mathematics

Many of you are aware that last semester, we entered a formal merger with the Society of Mathematics (SoM), another math club here at BU. They did not have SLIC recognition, which prohibits them from doing simple things like booking rooms, and the easiest way for us to help them out was to formally incorporate their club as part of our club. This did not affect either of our club's operations, and was simply a formal action meant to benefit another club.

This year, SoM has changed leadership, and the new leaders want to break away from our club and attempt to gain SLIC recognition. Thus our formal merger has been terminated, however we will still support SoM in their future endeavors.

SoM hosts the BU Undergraduate Colloquium, which is a series of talks given by undergraduate students at BU. The goal of the Colloquium is to provide students a venue for giving talks on topics they find interesting. Oftentimes very advanced topics are presented here in accessible ways, so it's a great place to learn something new. Many of our members also attend (and even give talks at) the Colloquium, so feel free to swing by sometime. More details can be found in the SoM Discord server, which is linked on their website.
